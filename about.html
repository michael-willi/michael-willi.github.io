Companies

Uh Oh November 2026 to present

Uh Oh is a zinc cold remedy brand with one job: be the obvious thing to take at the first sign of a cold. The product is a quick-dissolve tablet, and I am building the brand in the IU Innovates Incubator. The site and waitlist are live, and nothing has shipped.

I chose the homeopathic drug pathway that Zicam and Cold-EEZE use, because a supplement cannot claim to shorten a cold. In early customer interviews, people named DayQuil as their go-to, and none had heard of Zicam or Cold-EEZE. The method is the one the last two companies taught me: talk to customers before committing to anything, and let what they say decide what gets built.

Brine Prospect October 2026

Brine Prospect started as a mail-in lithium test kit for oilfield brine, paired with a lease value calculator that shows how widely a lithium right’s value swings with concentration. It grew out of my lithium thesis and was built in the IU Innovates Incubator. I built the brand, website, and calculator, launched a waitlist with analytics to measure demand before committing to inventory, and pressure-tested the model with industry contacts at a Houston energy trading firm and an independent oil and gas operator.

That operator conversation broke my core assumption. Operators already get full-panel water analysis, lithium included, for free, so a kit priced for them solves a problem they do not have. Finding that out on one call, before funding a lab partnership or raising money, preserved the capital the original plan would have spent on the wrong customer.

Before I ended it, I presented Brine Prospect at the IU Innovates pitch competition as the only founder nominated from the technology cohort, one of 20 pitches before a live audience.

Impact Margin April to September 2026

I founded and led Impact Margin, software that read a business’s operating and utility bills, flagged overbilling, cost spikes, and late fees, and showed the owner exactly where the savings were and how to capture them. In a few months my cofounder and CTO and I took it from an idea we were testing on campus to a live web app with a paying customer, a local coffee shop that came on as a design partner and shaped the roadmap with us. It was priced at $5 a month per location.

Repeated sales attempts never converted a second customer, so I shut the company down in September 2026 and moved my Incubator work to other opportunities.

Research and underwriting

Venture thesis: Reduce, Reuse, Recycle... Profit? June to July 2026

The U.S. has exactly one operating lithium mine, and the next big domestic supply might be sitting in oilfield wastewater. I went deep on that case and published a venture thesis, “Reduce, Reuse, Recycle... Profit?”, on the startups pulling lithium out of Permian Basin produced water. It covers the resource, the extraction technology, the competing operating models, who owns the water, and where value will concentrate.

I sized the prize from USGS supply gap data and Fastmarkets pricing at $2.5 to $6 billion of theoretical annual feedstock value across the price cycle, then made the strongest bear case against the thesis and answered it. It drew inbound from oil and gas executives and led to a shadow day on Rio Energy International’s oil trading desk in Houston. The second edition, published in July 2026, folds in the technical feedback industry readers sent, and Brine Prospect grew out of it. It is on my LinkedIn.

Multifamily underwriting April and September 2026

I have underwritten two live multifamily deals in Bloomington. In April 2026 I underwrote a 12-unit acquisition by adapting an AI-built Excel acquisition model with my own rent, expense, and debt assumptions: net operating income, capital structure, and levered and unlevered returns (IRR, equity multiple, cash-on-cash). I ran sensitivities on exit cap rate, rent growth, and price to find the breakeven and size the downside, wrote the investment memo with comparable transactions, then rebuilt the seller’s numbers. They told a different story than the offering.

In September 2026 I underwrote a 16-unit deal at $1.89 million, about a 3.9% cap, as an apartment-to-condo conversion, with a break-even near $170,000 a unit against target sales of about $200,000. I rebuilt the capital stack around seller financing on a syndicator’s feedback.

Roblox player retention study October 2026

On the advice of Roblox’s head of product marketing, I ran a branching survey of 59 Indiana University students on how they grew up with Roblox, how they play now, and why some quit. It is a convenience sample, so the numbers describe my respondents, not the platform. The common story is that players age out to console and PC. Most of mine did not. 33 of the 47 who grew up on Roblox had played in the last year, and 26 of those 33 play monthly or less. They downshifted instead of leaving.

What brings them back is people, not content: 15 return for friends who still play, and only 4 for a specific game. From that I wrote a three-part product and marketing play: fix the re-entry handoff for lapsed players, target occasional players before the ones who quit, and keep betting on a breakout adult game that spreads friend to friend.
