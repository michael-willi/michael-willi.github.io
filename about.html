Companies

Brine Prospect October 2026

Brine Prospect started as a mail-in lithium test kit for oilfield brine, paired with a lease value calculator that shows how widely a lithium right's value swings with concentration. It grew out of my lithium thesis and was built in the IU Innovates Incubator. I built the brand, website, and calculator, launched a waitlist with analytics to measure demand before committing to inventory, and pressure-tested the model with industry contacts at a Houston energy trading firm and an independent oil and gas operator.

That operator conversation broke my core assumption. Operators already get full-panel water analysis, lithium included, for free, so a kit priced for them solves a problem they do not have. Finding that out on one call, before funding a lab partnership or raising money, preserved the capital the original plan would have spent on the wrong customer.

Impact Margin April to September 2026

I founded and led Impact Margin, software that read a business's operating and utility bills, flagged overbilling, cost spikes, and late fees, and showed the owner exactly where the savings were and how to capture them. In a few months my cofounder and CTO and I took it from an idea we were testing on campus to a live web app with a paying customer, a local coffee shop that came on as a design partner and shaped the roadmap with us. Repeated sales attempts never converted a second customer, so I shut the company down and moved my Incubator work to other opportunities.

Research and writing

The same lens runs through my research and writing. The U.S. has exactly one operating lithium mine, and the next big domestic supply might be sitting in oilfield wastewater. I went deep on that case and published a venture thesis, "Reduce, Reuse, Recycle... Profit?", on the startups pulling lithium out of Permian Basin produced water. It drew inbound from senior oil and gas executives, led to calls with executives and a startup operator in the play, and to a shadow day on Rio Energy International's oil trading desk in Houston. The second edition, published in July 2026, folds in the technical feedback industry readers sent, and Brine Prospect grew out of it.

In October 2026, on the advice of Roblox's head of product marketing, I ran a branching survey of 59 Indiana University students on how they grew up with Roblox, how they play now, and why some quit. It is a convenience sample, so the numbers describe my respondents, not the platform. The common story is that players age out to console and PC. Most of mine did not. 33 of the 47 who grew up on Roblox had played in the last year, and 26 of those 33 play monthly or less. They downshifted instead of leaving. What brings them back is people, not content: 15 return for friends who still play, and only 4 for a specific game. From that I wrote a three-part product and marketing play: fix the re-entry handoff for lapsed players, target occasional players before the ones who quit, and keep betting on a breakout adult game that spreads friend to friend.

I have also underwritten a twelve-unit multifamily acquisition near campus from rent roll to returns, modeling its in-place performance against a value-add scenario with a full pro forma. All of it is on my LinkedIn.
