Software, CPG, and deep tech

Each interest traces back to work I have done.

Software

I am in the technology cohort of the IU Innovates Incubator this fall, and that is where my interest in software comes from. The founders around me are building software companies, and I get to watch up close how they build it, price it, and sell it. The cohort’s mentors, speakers, and pitch competition have given me direct feedback on my own work.

I have shipped software too, leveraging AI coding tools. With my cofounder and CTO I took Impact Margin from a campus idea to a live web app, and I built the Brine Prospect site and its lithium lease value calculator. What stayed with me is how cheaply software lets you test demand. A waitlist and page analytics can tell you whether an idea has a customer before you spend anything on inventory or a lab.

CPG

CPG is my undergraduate background. My degree is in Merchandising with a Product Development concentration, and product development is not just coursework but the discipline of turning an idea into a paying customer. I study it in class and run it for real.

From 2020 to 2024 I started and ran direct-to-consumer brands in apparel and sporting goods, sourcing overseas through Alibaba and setting prices and margins item by item. Now I am building Uh Oh, a pre-launch zinc cold remedy brand, in the Incubator.

Deep tech

My deep tech example is the venture thesis I wrote in the summer of 2026, “Reduce, Reuse, Recycle... Profit?”, on the startups pulling lithium out of Permian Basin oilfield wastewater. It covers the resource, the extraction technology, the competing operating models, who owns the water, and where value will concentrate.

I sized the prize from USGS supply gap data and Fastmarkets pricing at $2.5 to $6 billion of theoretical annual feedstock value across the price cycle, then made the strongest bear case against the thesis and answered it. I published it on LinkedIn, and it led to a shadow day on Rio Energy International’s oil trading desk in Houston. Brine Prospect was the company I started from it.

What I have underwritten and researched

16-unit condo conversion Multifamily underwriting, September 2026

I underwrote a live deal at $1.89 million, about a 3.9% cap, as an apartment-to-condo conversion. The break-even came in near $170,000 a unit against target sales of about $200,000. I rebuilt the capital stack around seller financing on a syndicator’s feedback.

12-unit acquisition Multifamily underwriting, April 2026

I underwrote a live 12-unit deal by adapting an AI-built Excel acquisition model with my own rent, expense, and debt assumptions. I ran sensitivities on exit cap rate, rent growth, and price to find the breakeven and size the downside, wrote the investment memo with comparable transactions, then rebuilt the seller’s numbers. They told a different story than the offering.

Lithium venture thesis Self-directed research, June to July 2026

“Reduce, Reuse, Recycle... Profit?” is my venture thesis on lithium from Permian Basin oilfield wastewater, with the strongest bear case made against it and answered. I released a second edition after technical feedback from industry readers.

Roblox player retention study Survey research, October 2026

I started this on the advice of Roblox’s head of product marketing. I designed and ran a branching survey of 59 IU students on what keeps players engaged and what pushes them away, then published the findings and three product plays on LinkedIn.

Growth Equity Masterclass

In progress. I expect to finish in December 2026.

The Growth Equity Masterclass is a growth investing course taught by Mike Hinckley, a growth-stage investor whose background includes General Atlantic. It starts with what growth investing is and how growth investors value a company, moves through a paper LBO and full LBO models, and then goes into the mechanics specific to growth deals. It ends with the sourcing cold call and the market thesis.

  1. Intro to growth investingWhat growth equity is, how growth investors make money, and how firms evaluate a company.
  2. ValuationA step-by-step valuation process, growth multiples, and value drivers.
  3. Financial modeling and case studiesThe modeling process, the core technical concepts, and a paper LBO.
  4. LBO models in growth investingExtending the paper LBO into a full LBO model, and how LBOs are used in growth deals.
  5. Growth-specific modelingPre- and post-money valuation, minority investments, primary and secondary proceeds, option pools, liquidation preferences, SaaS ARR and cohort analysis, and unit economics.
  6. Mock cold callSourcing, preparation and a script, and a cold call case study.
  7. Market thesisWhat growth firms look for, how to find and vet private companies, and a worked example.
